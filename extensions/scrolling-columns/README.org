#+TITLE: scrolling-columns
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

niri and PaperWM, as a container kind: an unbounded horizontal strip of
columns, of which a window's worth is on screen at a time, scrolling sideways
as you move.

* Using it

#+begin_src lisp
;; ~/.config/latticewm/init.lisp
(load-extension "scrolling-columns")
#+end_src

then, live or at startup:

#+begin_src lisp
(scrolling-columns:scrolling)      ; current workspace becomes a strip
(scrolling-columns:scrolling 3)    ; three columns visible at once
#+end_src

Every window that was in the workspace becomes a column, in the order it was
laid out -- nothing is lost, nothing is reordered.  =strip-width= changes the
visible count afterwards; it is the strip's zoom.  Moving left or right past
the edge of the viewport scrolls by one rather than refusing, which is what
makes the strip unbounded instead of merely wide.

* What it is made of

=strip= subclasses =sequential-container=, which supplies addressing,
insertion and removal; the module answers how the strip is drawn, how motion
crosses it, and where you land arriving at it.

Unlike the example it was promoted from, it also answers the state half of the
container protocol: =copy-node-slots=, =node-signature=, =serialize-node= and
=deserialize-node= carry the scroll position and visible count through undo,
through snapshots, and through save/restore of the session.  EXTENDING.org's
container-surface table names what skipping those costs; this module does not
skip them.
